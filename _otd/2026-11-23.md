On 24 November 1885, the Rye lifeboat was called to the aid of the fishing smack Maid of Kent. This incident is recorded in the service history of the station, which operated from Camber Sands for 44 years before closing in 1901. The rescue was part of a long tradition of maritime assistance in the area, following earlier calls to vessels such as the steamship Medea in 1882 and the brigantine Cyrus in 1871.

The station, initially named Camber Lifeboat Station, was established in 1857 by the Royal National Lifeboat Institution. It was situated to the east of Camber Coastguard Station and to the west of the village of Camber, on the east bank of the River Rother estuary. Over the decades, the station received several new boats, including the Mary Stanford in 1881 and the Edward and Lucille in 1893. Coxswain James Collins was awarded the RNLI Silver Medal in 1891 for his gallant services, which included helping to save 45 lives over a period of 12 years.

Today, nothing remains of the boathouses at Camber Sands. The final lifeboat, the Edward and Lucille, was transferred to Scarborough and then St Annes, where she was wrecked while operating as a boarding boat.
