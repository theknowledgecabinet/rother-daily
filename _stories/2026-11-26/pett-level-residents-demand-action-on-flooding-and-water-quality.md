Residents of Pett Level gathered at The Smugglers Retreat on Monday 23 November to press the Environment Agency and Southern Water for better management of flood risks and water pollution. The public meeting, led by MP Helena Dollimore, brought together representatives from Southern Water, the Environment Agency, East Sussex Fire & Rescue Service, and Pett Parish Council.

The discussion centred on the 2023 heavy rainfall, which caused the Marsham Brook Sewer to overflow and flooded 20 households in Marshambrook Lane. Southern Water stated its focus is on installing measures to slow water flow, including temporary pumps and household water butts. However, it was reported that the tanks at Fairlight Water Treatment Works have a capacity for 1,600 people, while the local population is now 2,360. Southern Water has no immediate plans to increase tank size.

Concerns were also raised about the lack of dredging in the Royal Military Canal and Marsham Brook for over three years. Mark Douch of the Environment Agency cited a lack of funding and stated that dredging was not the answer. Frank Langrish, a local farmer, questioned this position, noting that £35 million had been spent on the Rother Tidal Wall East. He also highlighted unaddressed issues, including the need for more groynes and the removal of fly-tipped waste from the sea road ditch.
