The famous clock at St Mary the Virgin in Rye is in need of urgent restoration, according to a report by Andrew Bamji and Roy Abel in Rye News. While the internal mechanism was restored in 2012 by the Cumbria Clock Company, the exterior fittings have suffered significant damage from weather, ageing, and pigeons over the past four decades.

The authors note that the wooden face and cartouche have split, rust marks are visible, and the paint on the Quarter Boys is peeling. More critically, the hangings for the Quarter Boy Bells are badly corroded and require replacement. The original wooden figures were replaced with fibreglass copies in 1969 after the originals decayed beyond repair.

Horologists consider this the most important parish church clock in Britain and likely the oldest turret clock still working in its original position. The clock has told time in Rye for nearly 500 years, with recent research suggesting it may date back to the 1380s, making it older than the installation date of 1561.
