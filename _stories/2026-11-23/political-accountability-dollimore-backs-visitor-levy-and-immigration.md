Helena Dollimore, the Labour (Co-op) MP for Hastings and Rye, voted in favour of the Visitor Levy (England) Bill Second Reading on Monday 16 November. The bill passed with 296 votes to 96.

Dollimore participated in all 10 recorded votes this week, missing none. She did not vote against her party's position on any occasion. Her voting record included supporting the Immigration and Asylum Bill at its Third Reading on Tuesday 17 November, which passed 290 to 94. On the same day, she voted against four new clauses proposed during the Report Stage of the same bill. These clauses were defeated, with results ranging from 44 to 368 against to 60 to 410 against.

The MP is a member of the Environment, Food and Rural Affairs Committee, the Statutory Instruments (Joint Committee), and the Statutory Instruments (Select Committee). She belongs to the Labour (Co-op) party.

Dollimore did not make any spoken contributions in the Commons nor table any written questions this week. However, she received three answers to questions she had previously tabled. On Monday, the Department for Transport responded to a query regarding the expansion of disabled persons railcard eligibility criteria, stating it aims to make rail travel more affordable. On Tuesday, the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs answered two questions. One addressed the government's stance on genetically modifying chickens to reach slaughter weight faster, noting support for voluntary moves away from fast-growing breeds. The other concerned steps to rewild natural landscapes to protect against extreme weather, with the department citing the Land Use Framework.

The Commons was sitting throughout the week, with votes recorded on Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday.
